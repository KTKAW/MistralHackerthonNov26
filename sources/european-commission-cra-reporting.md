# Cyber Resilience Act: reporting obligations

**In one line:** Since 11 September 2026, manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents in their products, starting with an early warning within 24 hours.

## What it says

- As of 11 September 2026, manufacturers are required to report two things: actively exploited vulnerabilities in a product with digital elements, and severe incidents that have an impact on the security of such a product.
- Open-source software stewards come under reporting obligations from 11 December 2027.
- Early warning: within 24 hours of becoming aware.
- Full notification: within 72 hours.
- Final report for a vulnerability: no later than 14 days after a corrective measure is available.
- Final report for a severe incident: within one month of the 72-hour notification.
- Reports go through the CRA Single Reporting Platform, which ENISA set up under Article 16 and which the page calls operational as of 11 September 2026.
- A manufacturer reports once. The notification goes to the CSIRT of the country where the manufacturer has its main establishment, and ENISA receives it at the same time unless exceptional circumstances apply. That CSIRT shares it with the CSIRTs of the other countries where the product is available, and may delay that sharing on cybersecurity grounds.
- The page links the legal text, Regulation (EU) 2024/2847.

## What it does not say

- What each of the three submissions must contain.
- How "actively exploited" and "severe incident" are defined.
- Whether the duty covers products placed on the market before a given date.
- Any duty to inform users, and any penalties.
